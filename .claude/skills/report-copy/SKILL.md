# Report copy: the pattern, not the numbers

Applies to every report on every branch, until Sandra says otherwise
(rule set 2026-10-29).

## The rule

The text beside a figure says **what the chart shows**: the overall
pattern, the message, the interpretation. It does not read the chart back
as a list of values. The reader already sees the numbers on the chart and
in the tooltip.

- Lead with the pattern: who leads, what is rising or falling, what
  changed, what stands apart.
- Use **one number at most**, and only when it anchors the message (the
  headline share, the turning point). Round it in words where you can:
  "about half", "around 60%", "more than ten times".
- Never list one value per series ("The Northeast took 21.5%, the South
  15.0% and the Midwest 3.6%"). That is the chart's job.

### Before / after (carta-demo, Figure 8)

- ❌ "59.9% of capital invested in Q1 2026 went to companies in the West, up
  from 50.3% in Q1 2023. The Northeast took 21.5%, the South 15.0% and the
  Midwest 3.6%."
- ✅ "The West's lead has widened: from about half of all capital in early
  2023 to around 60% today. The Northeast stays second, while the South
  and Midwest share what is left."

## What does not change

Interpretation is not invention:

- Every claim must hold for the **source's own printed values** (or the
  source text). Values digitized off a chart can draw it, but the copy
  never rests on them.
- Check each word against the data before writing it. "A clear second"
  is wrong if two series nearly tie in one period; write "second".
- Don't add sections, sub-chapters or text the user didn't ask for.
